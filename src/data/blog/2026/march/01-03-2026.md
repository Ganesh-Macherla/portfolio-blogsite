When I'm deeply focused...like genuinely either studying, or coding, or doing something in general, my brain puts almost all its attention into that one task only. It stops monitoring everything else, including its assumption of time. That's why hours can pass and I don't notice anything. This is a state, we Gen Z peeps call "locking in". But there's a twist to this whole shabang. 

The same thing happens when I'm scrolling brainrot endlessly, eventhough half of the stuff we watch are memes and chaotic internet nonsense, which aren't of any help to the mind and body one bit, but why?. It's not about one's prcrastination or efforts. It's about how the brain is wired to respond to rewards. Every swipe, gives the brain a tiny reward, a small dopamine hit. 

Let's get a bit more nerdy now.
Dopamine is a neurotransmitter, basically a chemical messenger the brain uses to signal motivation and reward. It's mostly produced in areas like the midbrain and released into pathways that reinforce our behavior. Dopamine isn't always about pleasure. It's also about anticipation too, you know?. And our brain just loooves unpredictable rewards, (you know like a dog receiving a chew bone after hours of waiting and neglectance showing through its small teary eyes, suffering because of lack of attention and when it finally gets some, its happy and over the moon, filled with joy after receiveing the tiniest amount of love)

NO that got dark, we're not doing that again, anyways.

---

That is why scrolling stuff online feels so addictive. The next video might be hilarious. The next notification might change my mood. The next swipe might finally be interesting. That uncertainty triggers what psychologists call a variable reward schedule, the same mechanism that makes slot machines (gambling hehe) feel more addictive. Because the reward is unpredictable, the brain releases dopamine in bursts. (Damn i did not know our brain is kinky like that, erhm ahmmm)
And every time it does, it strengthens the behavior that led to it. So our attention gets "locked in" again.

Our brain literally starts learning that "scrolling = potential reward" and it wires that loop tighter each time because our nervous system evolved to chase uncertain rewards...and historically speaking uncertainty meant opportunity. And opportunity meant survival.
And yes, this traces back to the same survival instincts our hunter-gatherer ancestors relied on.

But this time, the engagement is shallow. We're stimulated, but we're not thinking deeply. we're just reacting like, swipes-laughs-swipes-pauses-and swipes again. You see how we arent putting any effort to do these tasks that require basic motor functions!? and it also lasts for such a short time.

---

While dopamine keeps the loop running, the brain isn't forming strong, structured memories. Deep thinking activates the networks responsible for reasoning, reflection, and long-term memory formation. Rapid scrolling just activates reward circuitry, but it doesn't give your brain enough sustained focus to encode meaningful experiences. So in the moment, we feel engaged. But in hindsight, there's nothing solid to remember, that's why three hours can disappear without feeling real. Our brain wasn't measuring time. It was infact chasing prediction errors and the tiny gaps between "maybe this is interesting" and "okay, but what's next?". 

And here's where it connects to time itself. Our sense of time depends on memory density. Which means, the more meaningful moments our brain records, the longer that period feels. The fewer it records, the more that time compresses. So three hours of deep work feels full and three hours of scrolling feels like it evaporated. Maybe that's why childhood summers felt endless. Everything was new, difficult, and memorable. Our brains were recording everything. But now most days blur together because we're consuming too much and experiencing too little. And all this research about time and how the brain works around addiction, somehow hit me harder than the "+5 kgs fat and -10% hair durability" crisis I'm facing right now. (sad, ik)

Touching grass after learning about all this mess, made me realise that adults were wrong lmfao, time doesn’t move faster as we grow older, its just that we just stop giving our brains enough moments worth remembering.
